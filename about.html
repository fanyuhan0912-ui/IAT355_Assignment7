PROJECT OVERVIEW

About CRE Group’s EV visualization project

This project was created for IAT 355 – Data Visualization and explores how investments in electric vehicle (EV) infrastructure can accelerate the transition away from gasoline cars in Canada. Using a combination of spatial, temporal, and comparative visualizations, our website highlights the relationship between public charging stations, government incentives, and the rising adoption of EVs.

CONTEXT

Topic and subject

Gas-powered vehicles are a major source of air pollution and carbon emissions. As Canadian cities look for cleaner transportation options, EVs have become an important alternative. However, EV adoption depends heavily on whether drivers can reliably charge their vehicles where they live, work, and travel.

Our project focuses on the growth of EVs in Canada from 2015 onward and connects that growth to the expansion of public charging infrastructure. We look at differences across provinces and major urban regions, with a particular focus on British Columbia and cities such as Vancouver, Toronto, and Montreal. By comparing EV registrations to the availability of charging stations, we aim to show where infrastructure is keeping up with demand—and where it is falling behind.

OBJECTIVES

Project goals and user tasks

The main goal of this project is to make the impact of EV-related policy and infrastructure spending visible and understandable. By turning raw data into clear visual stories, we want to show how government rebates, charging networks, and local policies contribute to cleaner, more sustainable cities.

On this site, users can:

  • Compare EV adoption and charging coverage across Canadian provinces and regions.
  • Explore how the number of public charging stations has grown over time, especially in BC.
  • See how EV registrations and charging infrastructure rise together on a shared timeline.
  • Identify where infrastructure growth is keeping pace with demand—and where additional investment may be needed.

DATA & METHODS

Data sources and visualization design

Our visualizations are based on Canadian EV and infrastructure data compiled from government open-data portals and policy reports. Key sources include:

  • Statistics Canada – vehicle registrations and EV counts by province, along with trends in transportation-related CO₂ emissions.
  • Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) – public charging station locations and counts across Canada.
  • Transport Canada – Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV) Program – information on purchase incentives and national EV policy performance.

We clean and transform these datasets into a simpler structure with yearly values for EV registrations, charging station counts, and related policy indicators. The current prototype focuses on three core views:

  • A choropleth map showing provincial charging station coverage across Canada.
  • A bar chart tracking the growth of public charging stations in British Columbia over time.
  • A dual-line timeline comparing EV registrations and charging infrastructure on the same axis.

All charts are implemented using Vega-Lite and embedded into a custom-designed website. Throughout the design process, we emphasized clear legends, readable color scales, and explanatory text so that non-expert audiences can interpret the visualizations without needing to read the raw data.

AUDIENCE

Who this project is for

This project is designed for multiple audiences who care about sustainable transportation:

  • Government policymakers who need visual, data-driven evidence to plan future EV infrastructure and incentive programs.
  • Environmental researchers and students who are studying the connection between policy, technology adoption, and emissions.
  • Members of the public and potential EV owners who want to understand how infrastructure investment affects the practicality and accessibility of owning an electric vehicle.

By making complex policy and environmental data more visual and approachable, our project aims to support more informed discussions about how Canada can continue transitioning away from gasoline cars.
